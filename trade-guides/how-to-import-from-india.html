5 Phases of Successful Sourcing from India

India is the world's 5th largest economy, offering unmatched competitive advantages in agricultural products, polymers, pharmaceuticals, precision hardware, and specialty minerals. However, international buyers unfamiliar with Indian regulatory compliance often face hurdles. Following this 5-phase framework ensures smooth, transparent procurement.

Phase 1: Due Diligence & Exporter Credential Verification

Before initiating commercial negotiations, verify that the Indian exporter possesses an active Import Export Code (IEC) issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT), Ministry of Commerce & Industry. Confirm membership in the Federation of Indian Export Organisations (FIEO) or relevant export promotion council (APEDA for agro, EEPC for engineering, PLEXCONCIL for plastics). AmBright Global's DGFT IEC is 0512849182 and FIEO membership is FIEO/RCMC/2024/7731.

Phase 2: Formal Proforma Invoice & Incoterms Contract

Establish unambiguous contractual specifications: exact HS Code, purity/moisture/grade thresholds, packaging type, delivery schedule, port of discharge, and agreed Incoterm (CIF vs FOB). Specify acceptable payment mechanisms (LC at sight, documentary collection, or escrow).

Phase 3: Production Monitoring & Independent Pre-Shipment Inspection

Appoint an accredited third-party inspection agency (SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek) to draw representative samples at the stuffing terminal. The surveyor verifies quantity, package integrity, seal numbers, and conducts laboratory testing against contract parameters before issuing an official Clean Report of Findings (CRF).

Phase 4: Indian Customs Export Clearance & Shipping Bill Generation

The exporter's Customs House Agent (CHA) submits the electronic Shipping Bill via ICEGATE (Indian Customs EDI Gateway). Customs officers examine the cargo, inspect phytosanitary or laboratory certificates, and issue Let Export Order (LEO) permitting vessel loading.

Phase 5: Bill of Lading Issuance & Destination Customs Release

Upon vessel departure, the carrier issues Original Negotiable Ocean Bills of Lading. These, along with Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Certificate of Origin, and Inspection Certificate, are presented to the bank (under LC) or dispatched to the buyer via courier for frictionless customs clearance at destination.

Frequently Asked Questions & Commercial Guidance

Institutional buyer inquiries regarding specifications, maritime shipping, minimum order quantities, and payment terms.

How can I verify an Indian exporter's IEC number?
You can verify any Indian exporter's IEC directly on the official Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) portal (dgft.gov.in) under 'Services > View IEC Details'. Our IEC 0512849182 confirms our active status as AmBright Tech India Consulting Private Limited.
What documents are required to clear Indian cargo at my home port?
You typically require: (1) Original Bill of Lading or Telex Release, (2) Signed Commercial Invoice, (3) Packing List, (4) Certificate of Origin, (5) Insurance Policy (under CIF), and for agro products (6) Phytosanitary Certificate and Health/Lab COA.
